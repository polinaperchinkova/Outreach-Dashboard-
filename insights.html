The reply comes from the follow-up, not the opener

Across every sequence this year, Step 2 replies at 0.69% versus 0.51% for the first email — a follow-up on an already-warmed contact converts about 35% better, and bounces far less (1.4% vs 3.9%). By audience, Head-level contacts (0.92%) and Banking & Retail (~0.80%) convert best, while high-volume IT & Services and Financial Services generate opens but few replies. The pattern is consistent: fewer, better-targeted contacts worked through at least two touches beat high-volume single sends.

🏆 What's working
Step 2 is the best-converting touch. 0.69% reply vs 0.51% at Step 1, on 3,204 delivered. Follow-ups earn the reply — keep every cell at 3+ steps.
Banking & Retail lead on reply. Banking 0.80% (6,857 delivered), Retail 0.81% (2,720). These verticals justify more volume, not less.
Head-level contacts reply most. 0.92% at Head level (5,197 delivered) — roughly 2x C-suite (0.44%) and 3.5x VP (0.26%). Weight prospecting toward Heads of function.
⚡ Quick wins
Founders open low but reply high. 0.39% reply on 9,526 delivered despite weak opens — the copy lands when it reaches them. A sharper subject line could lift the whole funnel.
Single-step sequences leave replies unused. Any active cell stopping at Step 1 skips its best-converting touch. Add a follow-up to each.
NL Banks is high-volume, zero reply. 533 delivered, strong opens, 0 replies across the campaign. The CTA needs a direct reply ask, not a link.
🔭 Growth gaps
IT & Services is volume without return. 16,317 delivered — the single biggest bucket — at just 0.31% reply. Re-point that volume toward Banking, Retail and Heads.
Financial Services underperforms. 0.28% reply on 5,808 delivered. Tighten targeting before adding more.
Reply rate sits below the 2% cold-cell target. Blended reply is ~0.6%. The lever is fit and follow-through (stage 2–3), not more first-touch volume.
